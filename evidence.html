What’s changing

Things are changing fast, at every age.

AI is now part of everyday life, and it’s getting harder to tell what’s real.

  • 54%

    of UK adults say they use AI tools, up from 31% a year earlier.

    Ofcom, Adults’ Media Use and Attitudes, 2026

  • 64%

    of 9 to 17-year-olds in the UK have used AI chatbots.

    Internet Matters, Me, Myself and AI, July 2025

  • 47%

    of UK adults say it’s becoming harder to tell what’s genuine online.

    National Trading Standards, Censuswide poll of 2,000 UK adults, September 2026

National Trading Standards warns that old tell-tale signs, like spelling mistakes, can no longer be relied on to spot scams made with AI. Asking who’s behind a message, and why, still works.

National Trading Standards, AI Scam Watchlist, September 2026

Why a habit

Protection is improving. Skills are still missing.

Banks and phone companies are getting better at blocking fraud. But the tricks move to where protection can’t reach, and a habit goes wherever you go.

  • Scams that persuade are rising

    Fraud where money is taken without permission fell 5% in 2025. Scams that persuade people to send money themselves rose 19%, to £576 million.

    UK Finance, Fraud Report 2026

  • Not everyone is online

    About 1.4 million people over 65 in England don’t go online at all. Three questions work at the door and on the phone too.

    Age UK, September 2026

  • Families are the first line of help

    One in three UK adults (35%) helped another adult use the internet in the past year, most often a relative. Over half of those helpers have acted online on someone else’s behalf.

    Research for DSIT, GOV.UK, September 2026

Fear and hate

Fear spreads fast. So does hate.

Rumours turn into hate when a story points at a whole group of people. In summer 2024, false claims about who carried out the Southport attack spread online within hours, and riots followed in towns across England. Ofcom found a “clear connection” between posts on social media and the violence that followed.

Ofcom, reported by Euronews, October 2024. Some researchers argue social media’s role has been overstated.

  • Every age

    Of 544 people sentenced for the 2024 riots, 43 were under 18, almost a quarter were 40 or over, and the oldest was 81.

    PA news agency analysis, August 2025

  • Record

    Race and religious hate crimes recorded by police in England and Wales reached record highs in the year to March 2026. Police figures also reflect changes in reporting and recording.

    Home Office, Hate crime, England and Wales, 2026

  • 36%

    of referrals to Prevent, the government’s programme for people at risk of being drawn into terrorism, were children aged 11 to 15, the largest age group.

    Home Office, year to March 2025

Where we draw the line

It’s fine to be worried or angry about real problems, from immigration and crime to jobs, faith and how the country is run, and to criticise the people in charge. That isn’t hate, and 2ndScan won’t treat it as hate.

The line is a message that takes a real worry and turns it against a whole group of people. That happens across the political spectrum, in mainstream politics and media as well as on the fringes. So we ask the same questions of everyone, including the people we agree with.

2ndScan never labels people, and never monitors or reports anyone. We’ll develop this part with specialist partners from different perspectives, with a safeguarding review.

Questions that slow fear down

  • Who made this, and when?
  • Who exactly is “they”?
  • Who benefits if I’m angry?

The research

Learning to spot tricks can help.

2ndScan builds on research into “prebunking”: learning common manipulation tricks before you meet them.

  • 33 experiments, over 37,000 people

    A review of 33 experiments found that learning tricks in advance helped people tell credible news from manipulative news, without making them distrust credible news.

    Simchon and others, Current Opinion in Psychology, 2025

  • Tested on YouTube

    Short videos about tricks like scapegoating, using invented, non-political examples, helped people spot manipulation. They worked as well for liberals as for conservatives.

    Roozenbeek and others, Science Advances, 2022

  • Not everyone agrees

    A 2023 reanalysis found some prebunking games made people more sceptical of all news, rather than better at telling true from false. That’s why 2ndScan should be tested independently.

    Modirrousta-Galian and Higham, Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 2023

The three questions

Why trace it, test it, own it?

Each question is based on a well-studied way of thinking more clearly. They work on anything, from a scam call to a long-held belief, and don’t depend on spotting the latest trick.

  • Trace it: check what others say

    When researchers compared how people judged websites, professional fact-checkers did best. Instead of studying a site closely, they left it to see what other sources said about it. This is called lateral reading.

    Wineburg and McGrew, Teachers College Record, 2019

  • Test it: consider the opposite

    Asking people how they’d judge the same evidence if it pointed the other way reduced bias more than simply asking them to be fair and unbiased.

    Lord, Lepper and Preston, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 1984

  • Own it: step back

    People reasoned more wisely about their own problems when they looked at them as an outsider would. This worked for younger and older adults alike.

    Grossmann and Kross, Psychological Science, 2014

These studies were run in controlled settings, one task at a time. Whether three questions become a lasting everyday habit, for people of every age, is exactly what 2ndScan needs to test.

What we’ll test

What we think, and how we’ll test it.

These are hopes, not claims. Before 2ndScan becomes an app, or anything else, we want independent researchers to find out whether the habit works, for whom, and whether it lasts. We’ll publish what we find, good or bad.

  • We think

    A pause before believing, sharing or paying reduces harm.

    How we’ll test it: before and after a session, see how many people spot a scam and know the safe next step, then follow up three months later.

  • We think

    The habit carries over from screens to real life: the doorstep, the phone and the dinner table.

    How we’ll test it: include offline situations in every pilot, and see whether people use the same questions.

  • We think

    Families who share the same questions feel more confident helping, and older relatives feel more independent.

    How we’ll test it: short surveys with both the helper and the person they help, before and after.

  • We think

    Asking “Does this fit who I want to be?” slows fear-driven sharing, without making people feel judged.

    How we’ll test it: ask how likely people are to share a fear-driven post before and after, and whether they felt respected or lectured.

  • We think

    Practising the questions makes people more curious and confident, not more cynical.

    How we’ll test it: established questionnaires on curiosity, confidence and open-mindedness, before, after and three months on, to check people question more without distrusting everything.

  • We think

    Children who learn the questions check a second source more often.

    How we’ll test it: through teachers and parents, with consent and no child data kept.

Researchers and universities: let’s design it together.

Student projects, staff collaborations and independent evaluation are all welcome.

Where we fit

Alongside the good work already happening.

Valuable work is already happening across the UK. 2ndScan is designed to sit alongside it and point people to it, not compete with it.

  • Banks and phone companies

    Build scam warnings into their apps and networks, and they’re getting better all the time.

    2ndScan adds a habit that works whoever you bank with, and away from a screen.

  • Scam awareness campaigns

    Warn people about the latest scams and how they work.

    2ndScan adds questions that still work when the tricks change, and work on rumours and hate too.

  • Fact-checkers

    Investigate claims and publish verdicts.

    2ndScan adds the questions to ask first, and a nudge towards their work.

  • Schools and media literacy

    Teach critical thinking to children, mostly in the classroom.

    2ndScan adds the same skills at every age, and one shared language for whole families.

  • Digital skills help

    Helps people get online and use services safely.

    2ndScan adds thinking about what you see once you’re online, and offline too.

  • Not a scam checker. Not a fact-checker. A thinking habit.

    Where others give a verdict, 2ndScan gives you the questions and leaves the decision with you.

What’s next

Small steps, each one tested.

Each phase practises the same three questions on something harder. We only move on when real people show it helps.

Why start with scams and AI fakes? Everyone meets them, the stakes are clear, and many community groups already talk about them. That makes them a safe place to practise questions that work on much bigger things.

  1. Phase 1

    Now

    Build the habit

    Free sessions with partners such as community groups, libraries, charities and universities. A take-away card, and a practice tool: a web page that looks like a news feed, chat or email. We start with everyday messages like scams and AI fakes, and measure curiosity and confidence before, after and three months on.

  2. Phase 2

    Next

    Question what you’re told

    Rumours, headlines, and health and money claims. Practising lateral reading: checking what other sources say before believing or sharing.

  3. Phase 3

    Later

    Question “us and them”

    Posts that stir up fear or blame against a whole group. Developed with specialist partners from different perspectives, with a safeguarding review.

  4. Phase 4

    Later

    Make it everyday

    Schools, through teachers and parents, with consent and no child data kept. A phone app, if people want one and the research shows it helps.
